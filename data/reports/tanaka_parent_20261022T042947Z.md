## The specific thing that's hard

On quadratic equations, Tanaka keeps **flips the sign when reading a root from a bracket**. This is not a sign of laziness or confusion — it's a specific, fixable habit.

**What it looks like in practice:** In a question like <code>(x + 3)(x - 4) = 0</code>, the correct answers are <code>x = -3</code> or <code>x = 4</code>. Your child wrote <code>x = 3</code> or <code>x = 4</code> — the sign was flipped. The rule is: the root is the *opposite* of the sign inside the bracket.

This is one of the most common patterns we see in South African Grade 12 learners. It's not a knowledge gap. It's a habit that builds up over many attempts. It can be broken.
