The specific thing that's hard

On quadratic equations, Thabo keeps flips the sign when reading a root from a bracket. This is not a sign of laziness or confusion — it's a specific, fixable habit.

What it looks like in practice: In a question like (x + 3)(x - 4) = 0, the correct answers are x = -3 or x = 4. Your child wrote x = 3 or x = 4 — the sign was flipped. The rule is: the root is the opposite of the sign inside the bracket.

This is one of the most common patterns we see in South African Grade 12 learners. It's not a knowledge gap. It's a habit that builds up over many attempts. It can be broken.

What I'm doing about it

For this specific struggle, Thabo has been shown a worked example, told the exact trap in plain language, and given several variants of the same question to practice. Every attempt is recorded, and the pattern is tracked so we can see whether the habit is breaking or whether we need to try a different approach.

The tutor doesn't just say "you're wrong." It says "here's exactly what went wrong, here's the small rule that fixes it, and here's another question to prove you've got it."

One thing you can do at home (in five minutes)

When your child shows you a factorised question, ask them to say the sign out loud before they write the answer. “Plus three in the bracket — minus three as the root.” That's it. Two minutes, one question, done.

This does not require you to teach mathematics. It's just a prompt — a question that makes Thabo notice the pattern for themselves. That's how the habit actually changes.
